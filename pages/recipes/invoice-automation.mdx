# Recipe: Invoice Processing

**Department:** Finance / Accounts Payable
**Difficulty:** Beginner
**Time to set up:** 1–2 hours

<ConceptVideo id="recipe-invoice" title="Automating accounts payable with AI" />

## Overview

The Invoice Processing agent monitors an email inbox or document folder for incoming invoices, extracts all relevant data, matches each invoice to a purchase order, assigns GL codes, routes to the right approver, and logs everything in your ERP — all without human intervention for the 85–95% of invoices that follow standard patterns. Human reviewers only see exceptions.

## Use Case

**Who this is for:** Finance teams, accounts payable managers, and controllers at companies processing more than 50 invoices per month.

**The problem it solves:** Every invoice that arrives follows the same tedious journey: open it, read it, find the matching PO, assign a GL code, route to an approver, enter it in the system. For a small company that is annoying. For a company processing thousands of invoices a month, it is a massive operational drain — and a source of errors, delays, and missed early-payment discounts.

**Without automation:** 9–19 minutes per invoice, 19-day average processing cycle, frequent data entry errors, and early-payment discounts captured only occasionally.

**Trigger:** A new email with an invoice attachment arrives, or a file is dropped into a shared intake folder.

## Agent Architecture

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Email inbox / shared folder | Invoice intake point |
| OCR + vision model | Extract text and structured data from PDFs and scanned images |
| AI agent (Claude) | Classify, validate, match POs, assign GL codes, route approvals |
| ERP / accounting system | Source of truth for POs, vendors, GL codes (QuickBooks, NetSuite, SAP, Xero) |
| Approval workflow tool | Routes invoices to the right approver (email, Slack, or ERP-native) |
| Human review queue | Catches exceptions the agent cannot confidently resolve |

## Implementation Steps

<Steps>

### Monitor the intake channel
The agent watches an email inbox (e.g., `ap@yourcompany.com`) or a shared folder (SharePoint, Google Drive, Dropbox) for new invoice files. It detects PDFs, scanned images, and inline email invoices.

On detection, it acknowledges receipt by logging the invoice in a tracking spreadsheet or your ERP with status `received`.

### Extract structured data
Using OCR and a vision-capable model, the agent reads every field from the invoice:

- Vendor name and address
- Invoice number and date
- Due date and payment terms (e.g., Net 30, 2/10 Net 30)
- Line items with descriptions, quantities, unit prices
- Subtotal, tax, and total amount
- Remittance instructions

It flags any fields it could not read with high confidence for human verification.

### Match to purchase orders
The agent queries your ERP for a matching PO based on vendor name and approximate amount. Three outcomes are possible:

| Outcome | Action |
|---|---|
| Exact PO match found | Proceed automatically |
| Partial match (amount differs by <5%) | Flag for 1-click human confirmation |
| No match found | Route to human queue with reason |

Three-way matching (invoice vs. PO vs. goods receipt) runs automatically when your ERP supports it.

### Assign GL codes
Based on vendor history, invoice line item descriptions, and department, the agent assigns the correct general ledger codes. It learns from every correction a human makes — accuracy typically reaches 95%+ within 30 days of deployment.

If the agent is not confident about a GL code, it presents its best guess with confidence score and asks the approver to confirm.

### Route to the approver
The agent sends an approval request to the right person based on your approval matrix:

| Invoice amount | Approver |
|---|---|
| Under $1,000 | Department manager |
| $1,000–$10,000 | VP or Director |
| Over $10,000 | CFO or Controller |

The approver receives the invoice image, the PO match, GL code assignment, and a one-click approve/reject button. No login to the ERP required.

### Handle exceptions
Only invoices the agent cannot confidently process land in the human review queue. The queue shows the invoice, what the agent attempted, and what it needs from a human. Average human time in the exception queue: 2–3 minutes per invoice vs. 9–19 minutes for full manual processing.

### Capture early-payment discounts
For invoices with early-payment discount terms (e.g., 2% discount if paid within 10 days), the agent flags them as high priority and surfaces the financial impact: *"Paying this $50,000 invoice by [date] saves $1,000."*

</Steps>

## Tips & Variations

**Vendor portal bypass:** For high-volume vendors (top 10 by invoice count), create a dedicated intake email per vendor and skip the extraction step entirely by having vendors submit structured data directly.

**Duplicate detection:** Add a check against your ERP for duplicate invoice numbers from the same vendor before processing. Duplicate invoices are a common source of overpayment.

**Self-billing:** For vendors you pay on a consumption basis (utilities, telecom), flip the workflow: the agent generates the invoice from meter data and sends it to the vendor for confirmation rather than waiting to receive one.

**Multi-currency:** Add a currency normalization step that converts all amounts to your base currency at the date-of-invoice exchange rate for GL coding purposes.

**Vendor statement reconciliation:** Once per month, the agent requests a vendor statement, compares it against your ERP records, and flags discrepancies before they become disputes.

## Getting Started

1. **Define your intake channel** — where do invoices arrive today? Email is the most common and easiest to automate first.
2. **Document your approval matrix** — which invoice amounts and departments require which approvers? Write this down in a table before building anything.
3. **Export your GL code list** — pull the current chart of accounts from your ERP and provide it to the agent as reference material.
4. **Connect your ERP** — most modern ERP systems have APIs. Start with read-only access for PO matching; add write access for GL coding once you trust the accuracy.
5. **Run a pilot on one vendor** — pick your highest-volume, most consistent vendor and run their invoices through the agent first. Verify accuracy at 100%, then expand.
6. **Design the exception queue first** — before you build the automation, design what a human reviewer sees when the agent cannot decide. A well-designed exception queue makes the entire system trustworthy.
