# Recipe: Employee Onboarding

**Department:** HR / People Operations
**Difficulty:** Intermediate
**Time to set up:** 2–4 hours

<ConceptVideo id="recipe-onboarding" title="Automating the new hire experience" />

## Overview

The Employee Onboarding agent handles the entire sequence of tasks that bridge offer acceptance and a productive first month — IT provisioning, document delivery, policy Q&A, manager check-ins, and pulse surveys — without manual coordination across HR, IT, and hiring managers. The result is a consistent, fast, 24/7 onboarding experience that scales with headcount.

## Use Case

**Who this is for:** HR teams, People Operations leads, and operations managers at companies onboarding more than two or three people per month.

**The problem it solves:** Onboarding involves dozens of tasks across HR, IT, and the hiring manager — benefits enrollment, laptop setup, system access, paperwork, introductions, and training schedules. Done manually, this process is fragmented and error-prone. New hires often arrive on day one without a login, a clear schedule, or answers to basic questions.

**Trigger:** A new hire record appears in the HRIS or an offer letter is countersigned in the ATS.

## Agent Architecture

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| HRIS (Workday, BambooHR, Rippling) | Trigger source — detects new hire records |
| IT ticketing system (Jira, ServiceNow) | Receives auto-generated provisioning requests |
| Messaging platform (Slack, Teams) | Delivers onboarding materials and handles Q&A |
| Document storage (Notion, SharePoint, Google Drive) | Source for policies, handbooks, and guides |
| Survey tool (optional) | Sends 30/60/90-day pulse checks |
| AI agent (Claude) | Orchestrates the full workflow and handles natural language Q&A |

This recipe benefits from a **multi-step orchestration pattern**: the agent runs different sub-tasks (provisioning, document delivery, Q&A, surveys) triggered by different events on a timeline rather than all at once.

## Implementation Steps

<Steps>

### Detect the new hire
The agent monitors your HRIS for new employee records. The moment a hire is confirmed — ideally 2–3 weeks before the start date — the workflow begins. Key data it captures: name, role, department, manager, start date, work location, and employment type.

### Trigger IT provisioning
The agent automatically generates and submits access requests to the IT ticketing system based on a role-to-system mapping. For a Marketing Manager, that might be: Google Workspace, Slack, HubSpot, Figma, Notion, and VPN access.

This step alone typically takes IT 15–20 minutes per person manually. The agent does it in under a minute, and the requests arrive with all required fields pre-filled.

### Send pre-boarding materials
One week before the start date, the new hire receives a personalized welcome package:
- Welcome message from the CEO or team lead
- First-week schedule with calendar invites attached
- Benefits enrollment guide tailored to their location and role
- Equipment and IT setup instructions
- Key contacts and org chart
- Company policies and handbook (linked, not attached)

### Answer questions 24/7
From day one, the agent is available in Slack or Teams to answer any question a new hire has. It pulls answers from your document store.

Common questions it handles:
- *"What is the PTO policy for my first year?"*
- *"How do I submit an expense report?"*
- *"Who do I talk to about payroll?"*
- *"Where do I find the brand guidelines?"*

If it cannot answer confidently, it escalates to the right HR team member and logs the gap for document improvement.

### Keep the manager on track
Each day during the first two weeks, the hiring manager's Slack receives a short briefing:
*"Day 3 checklist for [New Hire Name]: Have you completed the 1:1 introduction? Here is a suggested agenda. Is their system access working? Reply YES/DONE to mark these complete."*

Managers who do not respond after 24 hours get a gentle follow-up. Persistent gaps escalate to HR.

### Run pulse check-ins
At 30, 60, and 90 days, the agent sends a 3-question survey to the new hire:
1. On a scale of 1–5, how supported do you feel?
2. What is one thing that could make your onboarding better?
3. Do you have what you need to do your job?

Responses below a threshold automatically create a follow-up task for the HR team.

</Steps>

## Tips & Variations

**Offboarding mirror:** The same architecture works in reverse for offboarding — revoke access, collect equipment, send exit survey, and trigger knowledge transfer tasks.

**Contractor onboarding:** Create a separate, lighter-weight flow for contractors and freelancers with a shorter provisioning list and a different policy document set.

**Multi-country support:** Add a location field to the trigger and branch the policy documents and benefits guides by country. The agent selects the right materials automatically.

**Buddy program:** On day one, the agent randomly pairs the new hire with an onboarding buddy from the same department and sends both parties an introduction message with three suggested conversation starters.

**Rehire detection:** Add a check against your HRIS history to detect rehires. They get a shortened onboarding flow that skips already-known cultural context but refreshes policies and tools.

## Getting Started

1. **Map your current onboarding steps** — list every task that happens between offer acceptance and day 30. Most teams are surprised how many there are (20–40 is common).
2. **Identify the highest-friction handoffs** — where does information move between systems? These are your highest-value automation targets.
3. **Build the FAQ document first** — the fastest ROI is training the agent on your actual policies so it can answer new hire questions accurately. Start with the 20 most common HR questions.
4. **Wire up IT provisioning** — connect to your ticketing system and create a role-to-system mapping spreadsheet. This alone saves 15+ minutes per hire.
5. **Pilot with one hire** — run the next new employee through the automated workflow end to end, then collect feedback before expanding.
